## Where to look next

**Three pins and a length of thread.** Lay a simple loop around the left two pins in a disk and trace its starting outline in ink. Keep the thread in the plane. Let A turn the left pair once around each other and B turn the right pair once in the same sense. Write [X,Y] for X, Y, undo X, undo Y. Repeat the motion [[A,B],A]. How few times can the thread cross the **ink outline** after each repeat if it can slide freely around the pins? The loop stays simple throughout.

The predicted minima are 256 after one cycle and 66,048 after two. In column slope coordinates one cycle takes the starting loop from (1, 0) to (465, −128). The crossing count is twice the absolute determinant of the two slope vectors. The braid matrix has determinant 1 and trace 258, so these counts obey $`I_{n+2}=258I_{n+1}-I_n`$ starting at 0 and 256. Their successive ratio tends to $`129+16\sqrt{65}`$, about 257.996. Removing any pin leaves the other two with the identity braid, so the thread can be moved back to its starting position around the remaining pins. The physical demonstration is a proposal. [Full-twist paths](../shape_search_20261121/extension/brunnian/README.md#local-maps-and-disjoint-trapping-tubes) · [Boyland, Aref and Stremler on stirring with rods](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/boyland/files/stir.pdf) · [Kida on intersection numbers for sphere slopes](https://www.math.kyoto-u.ac.jp/preprint/2005/14kida.pdf)

**An orbit that prints its own instructions.** Draw the two circles from the RAAR projection algorithm on a black-and-white grid. Read the grid row by row as a target sequence. A covering proof guarantees a periodic initial point whose orbit encodes that sequence. Each pixel takes 49 updates. The window visited on the seventh update of the block gives its color. The open experiment is to compute the starting coordinates for this drawing and see which pixels survive rounding them. [Window and return certificates for Case 1051](../low_hanging_20261122/algorithms/README.md) · [Binary coding for this map](../../docs/raar-symbolic-dynamics.md#consequence-arbitrary-binary-itineraries-and-positive-entropy) · [Hayes, Grebogi and Ott on coding a message in a chaotic oscillator](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.3031)

**Five transparent maps of one Newton cycle.** Give all five poles the same displacement. Paint one transparent layer per pole wherever that start reaches the nine-cycle. Use angle horizontally and logarithmic distance vertically. Two layers already overlap. At radius 10⁻¹⁴ and angle 27.5°, direct interval iteration puts poles 2 and 5 in the cycle and the other three at distinct roots. Can three layers overlap anywhere within radius 1.79568 × 10⁻¹⁴? One certified patch would settle it. [Pole numbering and verifier](newton/README.md#five-poles-five-different-roots) · [Hubbard, Schleicher and Sutherland on Newton basins](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~scott/Papers/Newton-HSS.pdf)

**One orbit or seven?** In Case 180844, rotate the 2T and 4T curves by one seventh of a turn. For each curve, try the seven time shifts $`jP/7`$ compatible with exact sevenfold symmetry, where $`P`$ is its period. The smallest maximum sampled separation is 0.130 coordinate units for 2T and about 1.75 × 10⁻¹¹ for 4T. Do the seven rotations of 2T trace separate orbits? Recompute the shorter branch near the jump in its recorded period to check whether the continuation switched branches. [Orbit data](../shape_search_20261121/flows/nested-skeletons.json) · [Wulff and Schebesch on following symmetric orbits](https://doi.org/10.1137/050637170)

**A flash on a surface with fifteen handles.** Send a wave in all tangent directions from one point. Color each patch by the route that reaches it first. A seam marks two equally short routes around the handles. When the straight-line mosaic jumps from 140 tiles to 280, does the seam network reconnect or slide smoothly through the change? Compare the same source at nearby weights, refine the surface mesh and trace the two competing geodesics back to the source. [Surface and mosaic](weave/README.md) · [Mitchell, Mount and Papadimitriou on shortest paths](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~cis6100/discrete-geodesic-87-Mitchell-Mount.pdf)

**Two knots, one coloring rule.** Unroll the four-strand cores of Cases 56014 and 114425. Both knots have determinant one. Number the colors from 0 to 4. At a positive crossing the upper strand keeps color x and the lower strand changes from y to 2y − x modulo five. Reverse this update at a negative crossing. Of the 625 starting palettes, only the five solid colors close on the first knot. On the second, 20 more close and each uses all five colors. Follow one of those palettes to the seam, where the returning colors must match the starting ones.
