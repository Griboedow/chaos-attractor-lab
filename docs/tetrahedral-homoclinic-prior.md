# Earlier three-dimensional tetrahedral chaos

**Tetrahedral symmetry plus three real flow dimensions is not a novelty claim.** This corrects an important omission in the earlier [literature follow-up](polyhedral-priority-followup.md). It does not identify our Gaussian-vortex trajectory with an older attractor.

B. Sandstede and A. Scheel, *Forced symmetry breaking of homoclinic cycles*, Nonlinearity **8** (1995), 333–365, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/8/3/003), [author-hosted paper](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~scheel/preprints/homcycle.pdf), study a three-real-dimensional cubic system in Section 3.1. Theorem 4.2 retains the proper tetrahedral group after breaking an additional reflection symmetry. Theorem 7.3 establishes an asymptotically stable geometric Lorenz attractor in an appropriate generic unfolding. Its proof uses symmetry identifications; Figure 15 is a connection diagram, not a computed attractor portrait. Section 4.3 gives a leading quadratic perturbation but leaves a further genericity coefficient implicit.

That is closer prior work than the six-real-dimensional Swift–Barany normal form or the equilibrium-based low-dimensional construction discussed previously. We do not infer from its symmetry reduction that one original-space dense orbit samples every tetrahedral sector. We also do not dismiss its theorem merely because it lacks a ready-to-run parameterized portrait.

## What remains to compare

Our [Gaussian-vortex candidates](tetrahedral-flow.md) have an explicit analytic field, a locally stable origin, constant negative divergence, a global absorbing ball, and finite single-orbit evidence covering all twelve sectors. These are useful properties to investigate together, not a demonstrated unprecedented combination.

The origin of the unperturbed cubic equation in that paper has derivative `lambda I`, with `lambda > 0` in the stated regime. Our origin has derivative `-gamma I`. Thus those distinguished equilibria have different stability types. This local calculation neither classifies the two outer invariant sets nor rules out an equivalence that does not match their origins.

The existing 108-regime shape catalog contains neither this unfolding nor Reiter's tetrahedral maps. Its favorable visual distances therefore cannot answer the closest historical comparisons. A future comparison must identify an actual earlier equation and parameter regime, distinguish its original state space from a symmetry quotient, and compare its individual trajectories with ours. Until then, the correct description is **an explicit and visually promising numerical candidate whose specific historical novelty remains unresolved**.
