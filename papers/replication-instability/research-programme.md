# Companion research programme

The periodic replication theorem is the completed manuscript core. These two separate questions are scientifically promising and retain their original numerical status.

## Hyperchaos in the exact source activity path

The [full23D continuation](../../docs/motor-activity-continuation.md) has six positive finite-time estimates through 20,000 observed normalized units at the main candidate. Source equations, physical activity mapping, full tangent basis, divergence identity, flow direction and row-pair distances have been checked. A six-positive spectrum is a property of one candidate regime, not a count of six attractors.

A numerical-dynamics paper should add a declared neighborhood study in physical activity and full initial state, with all outcomes retained; compare complete spectra under genuinely different integration settings; resolve any late clustering with transverse coordinates; and present the observed dependence of the number of positive directions on activity. An asymptotic theorem would need a trapping/invariant construction and a suitable expansion or symbolic-dynamics argument. More simulation time alone cannot supply that theorem.

The precise claim available now is persistent, independently controlled **finite-time evidence of hyperchaos in these equations**. The parameter neighborhood, basin prevalence and asymptotic attraction remain unproved. The two author theses also remain relevant to priority.

## Long switching and numerical synchronization

The [seven-departure record](../../docs/motor-log-seventh-departure.md) follows logarithmic contrasts over 280,000 units, including recovery from amplitudes beyond ordinary floating-point range. Certified periodic saddle nodes and local rates support a candidate switching graph; [the proof strategy](../../docs/motor-network-proof-strategy.md) records what is still missing.

For a mathematical network result, validate the connecting trajectories, the invariant spaces they occupy, and the required global transition estimates. Local Floquet ratios and a sequence of observed departures do not prove these connections or attraction of the network. For a numerical-methods paper, establish a global error argument for the logarithmic/physical coordinate coupling and compare against existing heteroclinic integration methods. The current tiny omitted-feedback bound is instantaneous, not a global trajectory error bound.

Visual priorities are signed logarithmic contrasts, event-time predictions versus observations, and an explicitly labelled candidate graph whose certified nodes and unproved edges use different styles. A projected closed-looking trajectory must never stand in for evidence of a periodic attractor.
